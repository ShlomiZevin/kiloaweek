The attached picture shows four screens of the app "קילו בשבוע" (Kilo A Week) that the owner picked - the app's design language. Design ONE more screen of the same app: a single phone screen, full-bleed, flat, no device frame, portrait. Hebrew only, right-to-left. The owner wants it cool, up to date and very simple: few things per screen, one clear main thing.

What the product is: a personal weight-loss method built on a calorie deficit over weeks. The user weighs every morning and every evening; the app shows how much the body burned overnight (yesterday evening minus this morning) and the weekly trend (goal: 700 grams to 1 kilo down a week). They can also weigh before and after any activity (same clothes, no wet hair, and they add what they drank meanwhile) to learn exactly how much the body loses. They log food by a photo or by telling what they ate (typing or a voice message); an AI counts mainly protein, carbs and sugars. An AI coach chats in Hebrew.

The app has a header that always stays at the top of every screen (the app name or the screen's name on the right; on the left a button to the AI coach and the user's own round profile picture, which opens the account menu) and a bottom tab bar that always stays at the bottom: בית, יומן, לפני/אחרי (the main middle tab), אוכל, התקדמות.

SCREEN: food, today. The day's totals - the main numbers are protein 86 g, carbs 74 g, sugars 18 g (calories 1,140 shown small). Three ways to add a meal: take a photo, type what you ate, or tell it in a voice message. "Again" - their saved meals as one-tap chips to add again (יוגורט עם שיבולת שועל, חזה עוף וסלט, שקשוקה). Today's meals so far as a short list (each with a small photo or icon, name, and its protein/carbs/sugars). Water and the count of sugar-free sweets (2 of 6) as one small line.
